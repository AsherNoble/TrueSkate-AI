# M1-HALFRES-20260928 — Does input resolution matter? (half-resolution probe)

Status: complete (2026-09-28). **Half resolution is not worse**; see Result.

## Question

[M1-DIAG](M1-DIAG-20260928.md) found the labels correct. Most Kansas City and
Los Angeles end misses are the model misreading a trail that is there, and the
largest group (13/30) is a faint or camouflaged trail tip at the 128×288
input. Would higher resolution help?

Stored clips are 128×278 and raw recordings are deleted, so a 256-wide test
needs recollection (~2 days of rig time plus ~$25–35 per training seed). The
cheap proxy is to go **down**: the identical recipe at **64×144** on the same
split.

## Design

- **Changed:** only `--image-width 64 --image-height 144`. The dataset
  resizes each decoded 128×278 frame with `INTER_AREA`; the fully
  convolutional encoder and soft-argmax heads run at any size (a local
  forward/backward pre-flight passed).
- **Identical** to good13100 seed 0: code `e9c237c`, the same shards and
  experiment manifest, temporal mixer, 40 epochs, batch 8, lr 1e-3, 16 base
  channels, seed 0, sequential decode, frame cache, L4. Run label
  `good13100_halfres_seed0`.
- **Test partition untouched** (`--no-evaluate-test`).
- **After training:** a validation autopsy and
  `analyze_validation_failures.py` for per-park rates.

## Decision rule (fixed now)

The metric is the mean validation recovery over the last 10 epochs, against
128 seed 0 (**85.32%**; best 87.63%). The 128 seed spread over the last 10
epochs is 1.7 pp.

| Last-10 drop | Reading | Next |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 5 pp | Resolution matters in this range | Plan a paired 256 pilot (~2k Kansas City/Los Angeles clips, trained at 128 and 256) |
| ≤ 2 pp | Within seed noise; not the bottleneck | Deprioritise 256; learning-rate decay next |
| 2–5 pp | Ambiguous with one seed | Offer seeds 1–2 (~$7) before deciding |

**Supporting evidence (descriptive):** whether the loss concentrates in
Kansas City and Los Angeles (128: 20.6% and 24.2% failure rates, against The
Workshop's 2.4%), and whether it falls on the end component.

**Caveat:** at 64 wide, the stride-2 score map has 32 x-cells (~0.031 each,
the whole tolerance). A drop mixes lost pixel detail with a coarser
localisation grid. Both are resolution effects, but a loss from 128 to 64
does not guarantee a gain from 128 to 256. A null result is the stronger
signal.

## Cost

Estimated ~2.5 h on L4 ≈ **$3.5**, plus the autopsy ≈ $0.3. Cap
`--max-hours 5` (≤ $6.6). If the guard falsely trips, resuming the same label
is safe.

## Result (2026-09-28)

`good13100_halfres_seed0` (code `e9c237c`, L4). The checkpoint payload
confirms 64×144. Epoch 1 took 700 s (decode and cache); later epochs took
~134 s, against ~560 s at 128 (4.2× faster). Total ≈ 1.6 h ≈ $2.1, plus the
autopsy ≈ $0.3. Evidence:
[failure summary](../evidence/M1-HALFRES-20260928/failure-summary.json).

| Validation | 128×288 seed 0 | **64×144 seed 0** |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean of last 10 epochs | 85.32% | **86.99%** (+1.67 pp) |
| Best (epoch) | 87.63% (30) | **88.70%** (30) |
| End / start / duration component at best | 90.8% / 96.5% / 97.6% | 92.6% / 95.7% / 97.4% |

- **Paired on the same 1,965 clips:** 78 clips are recovered only at 128 and
  99 only at 64. Exact McNemar p = 0.13, so the difference is not
  significant.
- **Per park** (failure rate, 128 → 64):
  - Kansas City 20.6% → 18.9%
  - Los Angeles 24.2% → 23.6%
  - Super Crown 13.4% → 13.1%
  - Skateboard GB 7.6% → 3.7%
  - The Workshop 2.4% → 2.9%

  The hard Kansas City and Los Angeles misses are unchanged by a 4× change in
  pixels.
- **End misses** fell from 181 to 145; they are still almost all short along
  the path (140/145).

**Decision (fixed rule):** the drop is ≤ 2 pp (it is actually a gain within
seed noise), so **resolution is not the bottleneck in this range. Deprioritise
256.** The camouflage explanation from M1-DIAG is not supported as a
pixel-count problem: halving the pixels did not make the Kansas City and Los
Angeles misses worse.

**Interpretation (not tested):** the encoder has one stride-2 stage, so
halving the input doubles each unit's field of view relative to the scene.
Lost detail and gained context may offset each other, or neither may matter.
Separating them needs an architecture change (more downsampling or dilation
at 128), not more pixels.

**Practical consequence:** 64×144 trains ~4× cheaper for no measured loss.
Planned experiments (learning-rate decay, subset scaling) could run at 64
for about a quarter of their costed budget, once seeds 1–2 confirm it is no
worse.

## Seeds 1–2 (approved 2026-09-28; rule fixed before launch)

Seeds 1 and 2 run at 64×144 with the identical flags (run labels
`good13100_halfres_seed{1,2}`, `--max-hours 4`), about $4. The test
partition is untouched.

**Rule:** adopt 64×144 as the default for development experiments
(learning-rate decay, subset scaling, architecture) if the three-seed mean of
the last-10 validation means at 64 is at least the 128 three-seed mean minus
1 pp. The 128 three-seed mean is (85.32 + 85.07 + 83.64) / 3 = **84.68%**, so
the threshold is **≥ 83.68%**. Otherwise, re-cost those experiments at 128.
Headline and deployed models are chosen separately.

## Seeds 1–2 result (2026-09-28) — adopt 64×144 for development

| Seed | 128: last-10 mean / best | **64: last-10 mean / best (epoch)** |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 85.32% / 87.63% | 86.99% / 88.70% (30) |
| 1 | 85.07% / 87.43% | 85.15% / 88.09% (37) |
| 2 | 83.64% / 85.55% | **89.18% / 90.94% (36)** |
| **Mean** | **84.68%** | **87.11%** (+2.43 pp) |

- **Every seed** is at least as good at 64 as at 128 (+1.67, +0.08 and
  +5.54 pp in last-10 mean).
- **Rule:** the threshold was ≥ 83.68%, and the mean is 87.11%, so **64×144
  becomes the default for development experiments** (learning-rate decay,
  subset scaling, architecture).
- **Cost:** about $4.2 for the two seeds, ~134 s per epoch each.
- **Test partition still untouched** by any 64×144 checkpoint. The best
  validation checkpoint is now 64×144 seed 2, epoch 36 (90.94%). Scoring it
  on test would be a second exposure of the M1-RETRAIN test partition, which
  is an operator decision.
