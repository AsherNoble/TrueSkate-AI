# M1-DIAG-20260928 — Why the good13100 model fails, and cheap wins

Status: diagnostics 1 and 2 **complete** (2026-09-28; results below). Experiment 3 is planned and needs its own
approval. All three use the **validation** partition only; the test partition
stays sealed apart from M1-RETRAIN's single headline exposure.

A clip is recovered when every knot is within 0.03 (normalised) of the command
and the duration is within 0.10 s.

## 1. Failure autopsy (validation)

`autopsy_failures` gains a manifest-backed path: sealed partition, the
checkpoint's own decode mode, park and command duration per clip. It runs on
each seed's selected checkpoint (validation only, L4).

For every clip, and summarised over the failures, it reports:
- **Which component fails:** start point, end point, duration, or a
  combination.
- **Error geometry:** along the path (early/late along the trace) versus
  perpendicular (off the line).
- **Evidence:** the distance from the commanded endpoint to the nearest
  rendered trail pixel. A small gap means the trace is visible and the model
  misread it. A large gap means there is no visible evidence there (e.g. the
  trace leaves the frame or is not drawn).
- **Where:** by park, device, command duration, and endpoint position in the
  frame.
- **Consistency:** how many failures are shared by all three seeds (a hard
  clip) and how many hit only one seed (noise).

How the results steer the next step (descriptive, fixed now):
- **Failures mostly one component and along-path** (e.g. end point short or
  long along the trace): a timing or end-head problem. Test the input's
  temporal resolution or the end head. More data is secondary.
- **Large trail gaps dominate:** the label is not visible in the frames. No
  model or data change fixes that; fix the collection or the window.
- **Scattered, seed-specific failures with small trail gaps:** variance. That
  favours the ensemble (2), learning-rate decay (3) and more data
  (M1-SCALE-SUBSETS).
- **Concentrated in one park or at the frame edges:** a coverage problem.
  Target collection there.

## 2. Seed ensemble (validation)

`evaluate_ensemble_validation` (new) decodes the validation partition once
and scores:
- each seed alone (sanity: must match the value recorded at training time);
- **primary:** the equal-weight average of the three seeds. It is fixed in
  advance, so its validation score is an honest estimate;
- secondary: the best 0.1-grid convex weights. These are selected on
  validation and are therefore optimistic; reported but not used to decide.

**Decision:** if the equal-weight ensemble beats the best single seed's
validation recovery by at least 1 pp, adopt ensembling for the deployed model.
It would then take its own single test exposure, recorded separately, after the
headline. Below 1 pp, it is not worth three times the inference cost.

## 3. Learning-rate decay (planned, needs approval)

The trainer uses a constant lr of 1e-3. Validation swings 3–5 pp between
adjacent epochs (e.g. seed 0: 84.1 → 81.5 → 86.7) and occasionally collapses
(seed 1: 72.3% at epoch 29). That points to optimisation noise, not a data
limit.

- **Change:** an opt-in cosine decay from 1e-3 to 0 over the 40 epochs. The
  default stays constant, so old runs reproduce. It is recorded in the resume
  config and the checkpoint.
- **Run:** seeds 0–2 on the full good13100 training set, same everything
  else, run labels `good13100_cosine_seed{0,1,2}`.
- **Metric:** the last-10-epoch mean validation recovery (mean across seeds),
  against the constant-lr runs' 85.3/—/83.6 (seed 1 to be filled). Also
  report the best epoch and the epoch-to-epoch standard deviation over the
  last 10 epochs.
- **Decision:** adopt cosine if the mean last-10 validation recovery improves
  by ≥ 1 pp. If adopted, the M1-SCALE-SUBSETS rungs use the cosine recipe
  (the full rung is then these runs).
- **Cost:** about the same as M1-RETRAIN: ~6.4 h and ~$8.4 per seed, **≈ $25**
  for three. Ceiling to request: **$35**.

## Cost of 1 and 2 (approved)

Four short L4 jobs (three autopsies plus one ensemble). Each stages the
650 MB shards and decodes 1,965 clips (~0.1 s per clip sequentially), about
10–15 minutes at ~$1.32/h. **Expected ≈ $1–1.5, at most ~$3.**

## Results (2026-09-28)

Code `e9c237c`, L4, sequential decode, validation partition (1,965 clips).
Evidence: [failure summary](../evidence/M1-DIAG-20260928/failure-summary.json)
(from [analyze_validation_failures.py](../../scripts/inspect/analyze_validation_failures.py)),
[ensemble](../evidence/M1-DIAG-20260928/ensemble-validation.json).

### 1. Failures are a directional end-point miss, concentrated by park

Seed 0 (selected) has 243 validation failures; seeds 1 and 2 look the same.

- **Component:** the end point is involved in 181/243 (74%): 129 end only,
  33 end + duration, 17 start + end, 2 all three. Start-only 49; duration-only
  13. On test, end recovery is also the lowest component (91.3%, against
  97.0% start and 97.9% duration).
- **Direction:** 175/181 end misses fall **short** of the commanded end
  along the path. The along-path error dominates (median 0.077, about 19% of
  the gesture's length); the perpendicular error is small (median 0.006). The
  model stops early on the line; it does not scatter off it.
- **Evidence at the commanded end:** 98/181 have a rendered trail pixel
  within 0.01 of the commanded end, so the trace is probably there and
  misread. 44 have none within 0.03. The trail detector is a colour heuristic
  that can fire on orange floor features, which likely explains the matches
  on frames 0–7, before the onset. Treat these counts as indicative.
- **By park** (failure rate): The Workshop **2.4%**, Skateboard GB 7.6%,
  Super Crown 13.4%, Kansas City **20.6%**, Los Angeles **24.2%**.
  - Parks are mostly single-device, but in Super Crown, which both devices
    collected, XR1 15.1% and XR2 11.7%. So park, not device, drives it.
  - Kansas City (3,784 corpus clips) has about as much data as The Workshop
    (4,029) yet about 9× the failure rate. Data volume alone does not explain
    the gap.
- **By command duration:** fast gestures fail more: under 0.45 s 21.1%,
  0.9 s and over about 8%. End misses have a higher median speed than
  recovered clips (0.62 against 0.50 screen units per second).
- **Frame edge:** not a factor (14.5% near the edge against 12.2% interior).
- **Across seeds:** of 421 clips failed by any seed, 128 fail in all three
  (a hard core, about half of each seed's failures); 196 fail in only one
  (seed variance).

### 2. Averaging the seeds does not help

| | Validation recovery |
|---|---:|
| Seed 0 / 1 / 2 alone (matches training-time values exactly) | 87.63% / 87.43% / 85.55% |
| **Equal-weight average (primary)** | **86.21%** (−1.4 pp against the best single) |
| Best 0.1-grid weights (0.8/0.2/0, optimistic) | 87.99% (+0.36 pp) |

**Decision (fixed rule, ≥ 1 pp): not adopted.** The end errors are bimodal:
a seed either finds the true tip or stops well short of it. Averaging a
correct prediction with a short one lands between them and still fails, so it
cannot fix a miss that is a choice between two positions.

### Implications (operator decisions pending)

- **The main error is structured, not random:** the end point falls short on
  fast gestures in particular parks. More data spread evenly over all parks
  is unlikely to be the most efficient fix. The M1-SCALE-SUBSETS curve should
  also be read per park.
- **Next, cheapest:** look at ~20 Kansas City/Los Angeles end misses
  (rendered overlays; free) to find the mechanism: a faint or fading trail
  tip against the floor, occlusion by the board or UI, or the game not drawing
  the trail's tip on fast swipes. Each points to a different fix:
  - a faint tip → input resolution or colour;
  - occlusion or no rendering → labels or collection.
- **Learning-rate decay (3)** still targets the ~half of failures that are
  seed-specific.

flagged the review as done while tired, so treat it as indicative.

| What the trail does at the commanded end | Misses (30) | Controls (10) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Clearly reaches it | 12 | 8 |
| Reaches it but faint or camouflaged by floor markings | 13 | 2 |
| Hidden (board, skater or UI) | 3 | 0 |
| Visibly stops short | 2 (one noted ambiguous) | 0 |

- **The labels are right.** In 28/30 misses the trail reaches the commanded
  end. The game draws the whole swipe; the model misreads it. The
  "game does not draw the tip" and "label error" explanations are not
  supported.
- **Camouflage is the largest single class (13/30, against 2/10 controls).**
  The operator's notes name white floor stripes that coincide with the
  predicted end. At the model's 128×288 input, the trail is a few pixels wide.
- **12/30 misses have a clearly visible trail** and are pure model errors.
- **Borderline misses:** the operator judged several misses practically on
  target. Across all 181 seed-0 end misses, 52 are within 0.03–0.05 and 129
  are beyond 0.05. The frozen tolerance stays; relaxing it after seeing
  results would move the goalposts.
