## Calibration search failure

In 073122 the automated calibration reported offsets +3.9 and +1.7 s and
rejected their disagreement. Human calibration onsets are instead near -0.63 s.
In 105440 it reported +3.2333 and +1.1 s; the two detections are near video
7.06 s, whereas the annotated calibration touches occur at 0.715, 2.848333,
and 4.981667 s.

The inspected calibrator extracts from command-relative time minus 0.5 s and
only accepts onset candidates at or after command-relative time. Actual
calibration onsets in these two recordings precede even that extraction window.
Its saved detections therefore do not correspond to the annotated calibration
touches. This is a concrete failure of its time-reference/search assumption.
The exact source revision used to collect every historical session has not been
established; the current code and saved rejection records support the diagnosis.
No fix has yet been implemented or validated.

### Controlled run01 human labels

All six starts now annotated: frames 38, 94, 149 (calibration), 212, 309, 400
(linear). Source export and timestamp-checked comparisons are preserved beside
its manifest. Visible-onset minus command-start offsets are 0.398, 0.640, 0.609,
0.876, 1.062, 0.877 s. Using the first calibration's offset would mispredict
linear starts by 0.478, 0.665, 0.479 s. No intervening collector operations are
needed for this variation to appear.

An exploratory alternative is onset = command-return time minus requested
physical touch duration plus a fitted recording offset. For calibration the
physical duration is 0.05 s, not the legacy tap payload_total_s=0 field.
All six alternative offsets span only 0.030903 s. Fitting on calibration 1 alone
predicts the three linear starts with signed residuals -0.009520, +0.008904,
-0.008825 s. Median of all three calibration offsets gives residuals +0.006359,
+0.024783, +0.007054 s. These are below one 30-fps frame, not proof of subframe
physical accuracy. The hypothesis was selected after inspecting this run and
needs prospective replication on new data. It differs from the retired raw
command-end anchoring because it subtracts each gesture's physical duration.
No alignment/training pipeline was changed. This relationship suggests variable
waiting before execution, but does not instrument or prove its internal cause.
