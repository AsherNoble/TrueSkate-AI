# M1-TIMING-20260912 — Human onset annotations and timing audit

Status: diagnostic evidence; no collection, decoder, calibration or training fix deployed.

## Evidence and scope

Asher annotated the first visible touch frame using the original-resolution
frame viewer, checking the immediately preceding frame. These are visible
onsets, not independently measured physical finger-down times. Frame indices
are zero-based; times retain FFprobe presentation timestamps (including their
nonzero origin). A fading trace endpoint was not annotated.

Small source manifests, unmodified human exports, timestamp arrays, provenance,
comparison results and training exclusions are preserved in
[`research/evidence/M1-TIMING-20260912`](../evidence/M1-TIMING-20260912).
Commands and labels were paired chronologically within touch type; reset labels
were excluded from that pairing. No research holdout was used as a software test.

All three originals are XR2, SLS 2013 Kansas City, recorded 2026-09-01.
045411 had neither an aligned nor rejected marker; accepted-training membership
is unverified. 073122 and 105440 had calibration rejection markers. These are
not a random sample of accepted training recordings. Most surviving originals
were rejected; successful alignment normally deletes originals.

Define offset = human onset video time - (command host timestamp - recorder
reported start timestamp). Negative offsets describe the mismatch between clock
references, not touches preceding their commands physically.

The groups in 105440 were identified after examining the data. The ~0.5 s shift
coincides with an interval containing accidental spin activation and a periodic
reset. Command-call overhead also changes from roughly 0.5 to roughly 1.0 s.
This supports changing command-to-visible-touch delay, but does not establish
whether reset, spin, automation scheduling, or another event caused it.
Call overhead includes both pre- and post-execution time.

## Calibration search failure

In 073122 the automated calibration reported offsets +3.9 and +1.7 s and
rejected their disagreement. Human calibration onsets are instead near -0.63 s.
In 105440 it reported +3.2333 and +1.1 s; the two detections are near video
7.06 s, whereas the annotated calibration touches occur at 0.715, 2.848333,
and 4.981667 s.

The inspected calibrator extracts from command-relative time minus 0.5 s and
only accepts onset candidates at or after command-relative time. Actual
calibration onsets in these two recordings precede even that extraction window.
Its saved detections therefore do not correspond to the annotated calibration
touches. This is a concrete failure of its time-reference/search assumption.
The exact source revision used to collect every historical session has not been
established; the current code and saved rejection records support the diagnosis.
No fix has yet been implemented or validated.

## Decoder and overlay context

Claude's overlay session found local OpenCV 4.13 delivered 29 frames where
FFmpeg delivered 31. FFmpeg evaluation of the existing checkpoint gave 79.59%
validation recovery versus local OpenCV's 74.30%; published validation is 80.00%
and published test is 80.05%. Residual cause is unproven. Only one corpus video
was hash-compared with Modal, not the entire corpus.
The shared loader is unchanged. Overlay FFmpeg decoding and the historical
independent image/time resampling do not establish temporal synchronization.
The first five overlays included test-split exposures; later ten failures were
selected from 256 shuffled training clips, not a prevalence estimate.
