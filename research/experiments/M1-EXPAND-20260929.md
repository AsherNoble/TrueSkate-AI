# M1-EXPAND-20260929 — Double the training set, weighted to hard parks, plus three new parks

park change is confirmed on the phone by the operator.

## Why

[M1-SCALE-SUBSETS](M1-SCALE-SUBSETS-20260928.md): validation error fell 39.7%
for the last doubling, so the model is still data-limited. Los Angeles
(21.4%) and Kansas City (16.3%) remain the worst parks. The operator chose to

## Analysis (2026-10-01, no new compute): quantity, diversity or split?

The expansion changed per-park quantity and park diversity together, and the
holdout differs from validation in both park and split type. These are
read-only analyses of existing per-clip results: four validation autopsies
(one per training size), the three 9,170 cosine seeds' per-clip results (from
M1-ENDPROBE's baseline, which matches the 9,170 autopsy on 1,965/1,965
clips), and the holdout. [Script](../evidence/M1-EXPAND-20260929/analysis_quantity_diversity_split.py),
[output](../evidence/M1-EXPAND-20260929/analysis_quantity_diversity_split.txt).

**1. The validation gain is explained by each park's own added data, not
by the new parks.**
- **Per park, failure falls 11–36% per doubling of that park's own clips**
  (4,585 → 9,170).
- **Each park's own growth in the expansion predicts its observed rate
  closely.** Los Angeles ×3.8 predicts 14.3%, observed 15.4%. Kansas City ×2
  predicts 11.8%, observed 12.8%. Super Crown ×1.6 predicts 7.7%, observed
  8.8%. The Workshop ×1.07 predicts 2.0%, observed 2.2%.
- **There is no sign of extra lift from the 3,013 new-park clips.** The
  original parks land about 1 pp *above* (worse than) their own-data
  prediction.
- **Skateboard GB is the one exception** (+14% own data; predicted 5.5%,
  observed 3.7%). Its gains against the three 9,170 seeds (7/4, 12/5 and
  12/6 clips gained/lost) exceed seed-only noise (6/10, 4/7 and 7/6). But it
  rests on about 6 clips out of 301, and it could equally come from the extra
  Kansas City and Los Angeles data.
- **Reading:** diversity did not measurably help the original parks; own-park
  quantity did.

**2. No detected dose-response with the number of shared training clips.**
- Each validation clip shares its recording with 2–10 training clips; in two
  long baseline sessions it is 70 or 133.
- Within each park, failure is unrelated to that number (permutation
  p = 0.64; the sign is even slightly the other way).
- **Limit:** no validation clip has zero recording-mates, so there is no detected
  dose-response; this does not rule out a step between "any shared recording" and "none".

**3. The holdout gap is not the gesture mix.**
- At validation's per-duration failure rates, the holdout's duration mix
  predicts **7.9%** failure; observed is **12.7%**.
- The excess sits in mid-length gestures: 0.6–1.05 s fail at 11–15% against
  3–9% in validation. Gestures of 1.05 s and longer match (5.4% and 5.7%).

**4. Holdout failures are not clustered in particular recordings.**
- Per-recording failure counts match binomial spread: dispersion 0.99× for
  Portland and Inbound, and 1.26× for Super Crown 2016 (p = 0.10).
- The failures are a park-wide rate, not a few bad backgrounds or board
  positions. This is consistent with waypoint drift giving each park enough
  positional variety.

**Overall reading:**
- The new-park gap is most likely a **park effect**: these parks have about
  1,000 training clips each. Within the original parks, about 1,000 own clips
  meant 3–26% failure, depending on the park.
- It is less likely a split artefact (points 2 and 4), though a step effect
  is not excluded.
- By the original parks' curves, doubling or tripling a new park's own clips
  should cut its failure by roughly a quarter to a half.
- **Value of the planned check:** scoring fresh recordings from the original
  parks would now mainly test the remaining step-effect possibility. Its
  expected information is lower than it was before this analysis.
